# Consent

An assistant reads an application only once its owner allowed it, and writes there only once its owner allowed that as well: allowing it to read never lets it write. A contract belongs to the application its id starts with (`mail` for `mail.emails.read.v1`); a `GET` contract reads, and any other method writes. The first time an assistant would read an application, or write in it, at a level its owner never allowed, the harness freezes the call as the model wrote it, stores it for its owner, and ends the turn with its own fixed question in its owner's language: nothing reaches the application but the preview of a write that offers one (see below), and nothing the model or a third party writes can phrase that question. The rights to call and to act through contracts stay above consents: an owner whose assistant may not act is refused a write as before, whatever they allowed. No consent is built in, and the organization agent, which acts for no user, never asks. Consents and waiting calls are rows of their owner, under row-level security. The assistants of the pilot kept reading Calendar when consents came, and writing there when writing asked too: every principal that could call contracts then got Calendar read, and every principal that could act through them Calendar write, so that accepting an invitation asks them nothing but whether to accept it. When reading Calendar came to cover the owner's events, private ones included, and no longer their free and busy times alone, every consent to read it was taken back, the pilot's as well as the ones owners gave themselves: each owner is asked again, in the words the contracts give then, the next time their assistant reads it, in a chat as for the availability check of an invitation that arrives. A request to read it that still waited, asked in the old words, expired, so that a yes to it allows nothing. When writing there came to cover declining the invitations an owner received as well as accepting them, a whole series included, every consent to write there was taken back in turn, the pilot's as well as the ones owners gave themselves, and a request to write there that still waited expired: each owner is asked again, in the words the contracts give then, the next time their assistant writes there. When reading Tasks came to cover the owner's projects as well as their tasks, and writing there creating projects, and assigning, commenting on and deleting tasks, every consent to read or write there was taken back, and every request to read or write there that still waited expired: each owner is asked again, in the words the contracts give then, the next time their assistant reads or writes there. When reading Calendar came to cover finding when the owner and other people are free, and writing there calling meetings, which emails an invitation to everyone invited, every consent to read or write in Calendar was taken back again, and every request to read or write there that still waited expired: each owner is asked again, in the words the contracts give then, the next time their assistant reads or writes there.

Either question names the application and says what its level, reading or writing, covers there, in its owner's language, as the catalog describes it: at the root of the OpenAPI document, `x-twake-domains` gives each domain a `name`, and what its `read` and `write` levels cover, in each language the harness speaks (`en`, `fr`), as plain words on one line (at most 64 characters for a name, 200 for what a level covers), addressed to the owner, without a final period:

```json
{
	"x-twake-domains": {
		"mail": {
			"name": { "en": "Twake Mail", "fr": "Twake Mail" },
			"read": {
				"en": "list, search and read your mail",
				"fr": "lister, chercher et lire tes mails"
			},
			"write": {
				"en": "prepare replies to your mail as drafts, which you send yourself, and move your mail between your folders, archive it or put it in the trash",
				"fr": "préparer des réponses à tes mails en brouillons, que tu envoies toi-même, et déplacer tes mails d'un dossier à l'autre, les archiver ou les mettre à la corbeille"
			}
		}
	}
}
```

A domain the document does not name in the owner's language goes by its name in the deployment's language, since a product's name rarely changes with the language, or else by its id, and what a level covers shows in the owner's language only, or not at all. The question stays the harness's own text, which the chat renders as Markdown: an entry whose words hold a markup character (``\ ` * _ ~ [ ] < > &``), anything that looks like a link, an address or a domain name, or a break of any kind is ignored with a warning, and so is an entry of any other shape, a key or a language code the harness does not know included, while the rest of the catalog loads; what goes in is escaped once more. The words are read with the catalog, at each refresh, so a new application needs no release of the harness.

The question asks for an answer in words, and carries no buttons: Twake Chat sends a reaction in the clear even in an encrypted room, so a tap on a button could never answer. Its content, encrypted with the rest of the message, also says which question it is, under `app.twake.assistant.question`: `{"id": <the request's id>, "expires_ts": <when it expires, in milliseconds since the epoch>}`, the id and the end of validity that `GET /v1/pending-calls` shows, so that a client can tell the question from other messages without reading its text, and offer both answers as words the owner sends; the text stays the same for any other client. The question of a meeting suggestion, proposed in the owner's room, adds `kind: "meeting"`, `room_id` (the channel it comes from), `with` (the other invitees, as Matrix ids their address maps to on the homeserver, not checked to belong to an account), `start_ts` and `end_ts` in milliseconds since the epoch and `title`, at most 80 characters, which a client shows as text, so that it draws a card from data rather than from the text; the question asked again after admission refused the yes keeps them, and a client that does not know them ignores them. Every question of the harness to answer yes or no carries it, the request to try again once the broker has the owner's permission, the question whether to answer for a whole series and the question of an owner's first brief about its reads included, unless its request no longer waited for an answer when the turn that asks it ended, decided from another client or closed; no other message of the assistant does. The owner answers with an exact yes or no as their next message after the question, in any language the harness speaks, or with a bare ✅ or ❌ on it from a client that encrypts its reactions; anything else they write is an ordinary message, after which only such a reaction, or an answer through the API, answers. The matrix role reads the answer itself, never the model, and only from an event that reached the assistant encrypted, sent by the owner: a reaction or a message written in the owner's name on the server, which cannot encrypt for the room, answers nothing, and an answer delivered twice answers once. A no drops the call and erases what it would have sent; the harness says so itself, without the model. A yes approves the call at once and queues a `resume` job with the owner's turns. The job grants the consent when the question asked for one, runs the call exactly as it was frozen, under the correlation id of the turn that froze it, records it in the session as the assistant's call followed by its result, erases what it sent from the waiting call, and lets the model go on, unless the call waits for its owner again, which ends the turn on the harness's new question; a resumed turn is admitted like any other.

Admission never answers a resumed turn by telling the owner to send their yes again. Refused by rate, a full queue or the platform's load, the turn waits as a turn an event woke does, tried again until `TURN_EVENT_MAX_DELAY_MS` after admission first refused it, and the call runs once admission lets it through. Refused as the owner's day is spent, or kept waiting past that delay, the call waits for an answer again, and the assistant says so on a message marked as the request, which asks it again and ends on how to answer, as any question of the harness does: a yes after midnight in `ASSISTANT_TIMEZONE` runs it when the day was spent, at once otherwise. The request counts as asked in the room from that message on, the room's latest question for an answer in words, yet supersedes no other request; delivered again, the yes that waited answers nothing. A newer request open in the room, asked while the yes waited, stays the one to answer instead: the request the yes answered closes as superseded, and the assistant says so without the marker. A request that ends before the refusal lifts closes as expired, and the assistant says why, without the marker.

Besides each application, an owner gives the platform's token broker, once, the permission for their assistant to act for them at all. When the broker has none, or the one it had expired, the gateway answers a contract call with the broker's 401, an RFC 9457 problem whose `code` is `delegation_missing` or `delegation_expired`. The call then waits for its owner, for the reason `delegation`, and the turn ends on the harness's own request, whatever the model would have said: what the call was about to do, in the application named as for a first use, why it waits, where to give that permission, and how to answer, as any question of the harness asks. The only link it shows is the broker's consent link the deployment sets in `BROKER_CONSENT_URL` (checked at start), never the `consent_url` of the answer, which a contract could write as well; without that setting, the request says why and offers to try again, with no link. The model's view of the call records that it waited for that permission. The owner's yes runs the frozen call again, once, and grants no consent; a call the broker refuses again ends the resumed turn on a new request. The organization agent, which acts for no user, reads that refusal as data.

Twake Space also needs one of its owner's API tokens, which they give on the broker's Twake Space step and which the broker holds beside that permission. A call to its contracts is refused with the broker's 401 whose `code` is `space_token_missing` while the broker holds none, with the contract's 401 `space_token_rejected` when Space no longer accepts the one it holds (expired, revoked, or of an account that left the organization), and with the contract's 403 `space_scope_missing` when that token lacks the scope the call needs, which the problem names in its `scope`. Such a call waits for its owner as for that permission, for the same reason `delegation`, and its request tells why: no token given yet, one Space no longer accepts, or one short of a permission, named in Space's own words for `space:read`, `space:write`, `members:write` and `feed:read`, and in general words for any other scope, which the request never shows. Its link is the same consent link, opened on the broker's Twake Space step (`app=space`). Each code is a refusal only with its own status; the model reads any other answer as data.

Five days or less before that permission expires, its owner reads so in their assistant's room. Every day at `DELEGATION_REMINDER_HOUR`, 9 by default, on the wall clock of `ASSISTANT_TIMEZONE`, the worker role asks the broker, one owner after the other, what it holds of the permission of each owner whose assistant is in its room: a `GET` of `delegation` under `APISIX_BASE_URL` and then `CONTRACTS_BASE_PATH`, naming the owner as a contract call does. The gateway must publish the broker's route there: at its root, `/delegation`, for a harness whose `APISIX_BASE_URL` ends in `/b2b/` and whose `CONTRACTS_BASE_PATH` is `..`. The worker role asks during that hour only: a pass that fails runs again within it, and a role that starts after it, or whose pass fails throughout it, waits for the next day's, which misses nobody, a permission being reminded of from five days before it expires. An owner whose permission has not expired, and expires within five days of the calendar, as the zone of their calendar counts them (see Time zone), reads when it expires, on the wall clock of that zone, and where to renew it: the consent link the deployment sets in `BROKER_CONSENT_URL`, bound to them as any consent link the harness shows, never the `consent_url` of the broker's answer. Without that setting, the worker role reminds nobody, and its line `delegation reminders off` says so once at its start. The reminder asks nothing, and nothing waits for an answer. It goes out once for each date the owner gave their permission, kept with the message it queues in place of the one kept for the permission before, so that the harness keeps one by owner at most: a pass that runs again, after a restart or on another replica, reminds nobody twice, and the permission they give next is reminded of in its turn. An owner of whom the broker holds no permission, which it answers with a 404 whose `code` is `delegation_missing`, or an expired one, is reminded of nothing, and no line says so. Nor is one the broker could not be asked about, and the pass goes on with the next owner: any other 404 is the gateway's, which publishes no route there, and the warning `delegation route missing` names the path asked; a broker that fails or does not answer within `CONTRACTS_TIMEOUT_MS` gets the warning `delegation reminder skipped`.

A calendar contract answers an invitation that repeats, or whose copy holds several occurrences of a series, only for the whole series, once the owner said so: a call that accepts or declines one without `series` set to true in its body gets a 409, an RFC 9457 problem whose `code` is `recurring_invitation`, and nothing is written. A copy that holds one occurrence alone is answered as an event, `series` or not, and an invitation whose event the organizer cancelled gets a 409 whose `code` is `invitation_cancelled` before the series is checked, which the model reads as data, with no question about a series. The model cannot answer for a whole series itself: from every new call for an owner to a contract whose body takes `series`, the model's or a direct one through `POST /v1/tool`, the harness takes `series` out of the body, whatever its value, so that only the owner's yes to its question sets it. When the contract's body takes `series`, a refusal whose `code` is `recurring_invitation` ends the turn on the harness's own question, whatever the model would have said. The call for every occurrence, its body's `series` set to true, waits for its owner, for the reason `series`, and is asked about as any call that waits: the request shows what the model wrote alongside the call, quoted, the question whether to answer for the whole series, in the application named as the catalog does, that call, whole, or, when the application offers a preview, what it says the call would do in its place, whose digest the call then carries, and how to answer; the conversation keeps that call in place of what the application said, for the model's later turns. The model's view of the call records that it waited for that answer. The owner's yes runs that call the usual way of writes, and grants no consent: at once when nothing else holds it, and otherwise once they answered, on a request of its own, what else a write waits for, such as a first write in the application, or a write a turn an event started prepared, which asks each time, even once they let their assistant write there; their no drops it, and nothing is written. That call keeps `series` through a request for the broker's permission that may follow, whose yes asks nothing about the series again, whether the broker refused that call or, after the owner's yes to the series, the preview of a request that still applies, such as their consent to a first write. A call whose preview for the question about the series the broker refuses, before that yes, waits for that permission without `series`: once the owner gave it, the contract refuses the call for one occurrence again, and the question comes then. A contract whose body does not take `series`, a call for the whole series it refuses all the same, and the organization agent, which has nobody to ask and whose calls keep the `series` they set, leave the refusal to the model, as data.

A question expires after `CONSENT_REQUEST_LIFETIME_MS`, a day by default, asked again by a refused yes or not, and a newer question in the same room supersedes it: an answer to either runs nothing, and only the first answer gets a notice; what the call would have sent is erased. Every hour, the worker role expires the questions nobody answered.

The creator asks the same way before it deletes an assistant. `/delete` deletes nothing yet: it asks the owner, in their language, to confirm the deletion of the assistant it names, saying what the deletion erases, and marks that question with an id of its own, which no pending call shows, and an `expires_ts` ten minutes after it asked; without an assistant, it answers as before and asks nothing. The owner's next message answers: an exact yes, in any language the harness speaks, deletes that assistant, and any other message, a command, an image or a file included, keeps it, the creator saying the deletion is cancelled. The question stands for the ten minutes, and only while the assistant it named is the owner's: once they are over, or once that assistant is gone, deleted through the API for instance, even if the owner created another since, a yes or a no deletes nothing and is told so, with how to ask again, while anything else is read as if nothing had been asked. The creator takes the answer as it takes any command (see The owner's sessions): a reaction on its question answers nothing, and the question takes one answer: of two that reach it together, the same one delivered twice included, the creator acts on the first and says nothing of the other.

A deletion, by that yes or by `DELETE /v1/assistants/me`, which asks nothing, erases what the harness keeps of the assistant, under its owner's principal and in one transaction: the owner's sessions, its memory, their skills and the ones it proposed, their consents, the calls that wait for their answer, the suggestions it made them (see Suggestions), the brief that waits for their answer, the question of their first brief, whether their brief still asks for its reads, when they were last seen in its room and what they chose of their brief, its time, its days, a pause or a stop (see Morning brief), the zone of their calendar (see Time zone), the activities their quiet hours hold (see Quiet hours), the reminders of their delegation, what it told them of their sessions and the question it asked them about their identity, with their answer. So go the jobs that would still run for it, the owner's turns, queued or deferred, an event's included, the suggestions it was still to make, with the messages they quote, those that would show its name, and what it was to send, and those of theirs that failed for good, whose content may quote their words; another owner's jobs stay. A turn that runs through the deletion keeps nothing: what it would keep in its conversation's name, a note, a proposed skill or a call that waits for the owner, finds the conversation gone, and its answer, which would go out as an assistant that left the room, goes nowhere. Nor does a turn of a room that waited for the owner's turn before it: it opens no conversation once the assistant no longer answers there, and a deletion that comes while it opens one waits for it, then erases it too. The assistant first leaves every room it is in: those it answered its owner in, the rooms they opened with it included, and any other the homeserver lists for it, such as a room it could not leave once someone else came in. Should the homeserver refuse, nothing is erased: the line `assistant not deleted: a room was not left` names the room, and the owner, told their request failed, may ask again. Its record stays, marked deleted, with the language its owner chose. What a new assistant needs or must not lose stays: its Matrix account, device and keys, as a Matrix identifier is never reused; the owner's quota counters, so that a deletion never gives a spent day back; whether they want suggestions and the rooms they muted, which they chose for themselves; the identity the harness pinned for the owner, and the one it saw last; the wake-ups, an event id and a date each, and the owner's listening journal, until its purge, so that an event replayed within their retention wakes no new assistant, and that the next one has no second brief of a date that had one; and, likewise, the owner's words it received and when it first read each session they came from, so that a copy of their words, or the words of a session older than those it remembers, count no more for the next assistant than for the one before. The gateway keeps its audit. An assistant created again takes the same Matrix identifier, with nothing of the one before, greets its owner in a new room, tells them there again what it would say of a session of theirs, asks them there again about an identity of theirs it does not know, and reminds them, five days or less before it expires, of the permission they give again for it to act for them, since the deletion revokes the one before.

A deletion also asks the broker to revoke that permission, by a `revoke` job the same transaction queues once the owner's jobs are erased, which no erasure of their jobs takes, that of their next assistant included: the deletion never waits for the broker. The matrix role runs it through the gateway, naming the owner as a contract call does: a `GET` of `delegation`, as the reminders ask, then a `DELETE` of the same route, which the gateway must publish beside it. The broker erases the permission, if it holds one, then leaves the owner's Drive instance, and answers with a 204: the line `delegation revoked` says so. A permission the broker dates after the owner asked for the deletion when a try asks, one they gave again since, stays, with the line `delegation kept: given again since the deletion`; one they give between that `GET` and the `DELETE` that follows it is revoked. One the broker no longer holds, a 404 whose `code` is `delegation_missing`, is revoked all the same, as a try whose Drive instance did not answer erased it before the broker could leave the instance. A broker that fails, such as its 502 `drive_unavailable` when that instance does not answer, or does not answer within `CONTRACTS_TIMEOUT_MS`, and a gateway that publishes no route there, any other 404, fail the try: the queue tries again a minute later, then after ten minutes, an hour, six hours and a day, and gives up after the sixth try, about a day and a half after the deletion, where any other job gives up after its third, within seconds. Each failed try is logged as `job failed`, of kind `revoke`, with why. So a revocation outlasts an outage of the broker, of the gateway or of the owner's Drive instance, and a gateway that publishes the route only after the harness starts. The revocations of one owner run one at a time, in the order of their deletions.

The owner asks their assistant what it may access, and the model answers from `consents_list`: each application and level the owner allowed, how and when. The owner tells it to stop using an application, or only to stop writing there, and the model calls `consents_withdraw` with the application and, for writing alone, the `write` level: the next call there asks the owner again, on every replica at once, since every check reads the database. A name the catalog does not offer is refused, with the applications it does offer, unless the owner had allowed it before the catalog dropped it. The withdrawal also closes the questions still open there and the calls the owner allowed that have not run yet, as a newer question closes an older one: an answer to such a question gets the notice, and nothing runs there after the withdrawal. No tool grants a consent; only the owner's own answer does. Withdrawing takes the `consents.withdraw_own` right, which every principal that can chat holds and a turn an event started never does: an event's text cannot make the assistant withdraw anything. In the owner's own turns, text the assistant reads, a mail for instance, could still lead it to withdraw a consent, which only makes it ask again. The organization agent, which holds no consent, neither lists nor withdraws one.

A high-risk write, one that sends words to other people in the owner's name, shares or publishes, deletes for good, or trains what other users share, waits for its owner every time, whatever they allowed, and runs only once they confirmed that very call. One request asks about every reason a call waits for, but the broker's permission and the whole series, which each come on a request of their own, so that a first write that answers for a whole series asks twice: the first high-risk write in an application asks once, and its yes both allows writing there and confirms that call, whereas a yes that only confirms a call allows nothing more; should its owner take writing back before they confirm, the call waits again, asking for both, and the request they answered is closed as superseded, as it is when the broker refuses a call again.

A write the owner's message may not ask for, as the decision model read it (see Architecture, Turns), waits for its owner in the same way, whatever they allowed, with the reason `unsure`: its request says that the assistant is not sure the message asks for it, unless it is the first write in that application, whose request asks to allow writing there.

A turn an event started reads as any turn does, its first read of an application asking as it would in a conversation, but every write it prepares waits for its owner, whatever they allowed, since what arrived was written by someone else: what the model wrote alongside the call, quoted in the request, carries the event's context. The owner's yes runs that very call and nothing more. The turn it resumes is still the event's, as if it had never stopped: any other write it prepares asks again, and it never changes how the assistant speaks to its owner, keeps a note or a skill proposal, or withdraws a consent, which only the owner's own turns do. A write that is also the first in its application asks both in one request, whose yes also allows writing there.

A request about a first use, a high-risk write or a write that a turn an event started prepared shows, in order: what the model wrote alongside the call, quoted under the harness's label ("Your assistant wrote:"), at most 2,000 characters of it; the harness's question, which names the application and, for a first use, says what the level covers there; the call itself, whole, as the model wrote it, in a code block, or, for a write that offers a preview, what its application says it would do, in its place, quoted under the harness's label ("Twake Mail describes it as:"); and how to answer. A first read shows no call: its question asks only whether its owner allows the assistant to read in the application, and says what reading covers there, since their yes lets it read there whatever it reads. A write the model wrote without arguments never shows its owner an empty `{}`: unless a summary stands in its place, a first write shows no call, its question asking only whether its owner allows it, since it already says what writing covers there, and a question about that very call, a high-risk write's or an event turn's, shows the tool it calls instead, as the harness names it. The harness lays the message out itself, as plain text and as HTML. What the model wrote renders its Markdown, held whole in the quote under the harness's label, every tag of it closed within it, but with nothing that acts, since Twake Chat opens a link on a touch without asking and a received mail may have phrased those words: none of its HTML, a link as its text alone, an address or an e-mail address as text, an image as its description, which no client then fetches from another server, and a heading as a paragraph. It shows as its lines of text should its rendering take more of the event than those escaped lines could, 12,000 bytes, and the plain text quotes it line by line. The question renders from the harness's own Markdown, and the call or its application's summary, which may hold what a third party wrote, shows as code: nothing the model or a third party wrote passes for the harness's words. An owner is never asked about a call they cannot see whole: a call, or a summary, that would take more than 16 KiB of the event that carries the request, as plain text and as HTML together, escaped as the event's JSON holds them, waits for nothing, and the model reads that it is too large to confirm, so that it may make it smaller; a first read, which shows none, never is. With the model's words, 12,000 bytes at most in each, the largest request stays under 60 KiB once encrypted, well within the 64 KiB a Matrix event may take. A request travels in its parts, the question apart from the call, and the API answers a turn with its plain text. The conversation keeps the request for the model's later turns as its owner read it, with the call as the model wrote it in place of what an application said of it: that is the application's data, which may hold what a third party wrote, possibly from an application the owner never let the assistant read, and only the owner reads it, in the request itself, which the harness stores nowhere else. A first read is kept as its owner read it, without a call, and a call without arguments without an empty one: what would run comes just before the request in the conversation, as the model's own call or, for the harness's own check of an invitation's slot, in what the model is told; but where its owner read a summary, the conversation shows the call as the model wrote it, `{}`, as it does for any call with a preview.

When the application can tell what a write would affect, such as the recipients a mail would reach or the item it would delete, its owner sees that rather than the call the model wrote. Before it asks about a write whose operation declares `x-twake-preview: true`, the harness calls the contract as the call would go, same method, path, query and body, in the owner's name and under the correlation id of the turn, with `x-twake-preview: true` and the owner's language in `accept-language`. The contract answers what the call would do, and does nothing: a `200` that carries `x-twake-preview: true` back in its own headers, whose JSON body holds a `summary`, plain text for the owner, on several lines if need be, without any control or format character but line feeds and tabs, and a `digest`, an opaque token of 1 to 256 letters, digits or `+ / = . _ : -` that covers what the call acts on:

```json
{
	"summary": "To: Paul Martin <paul@example.com> and the 3 members of finance\nSubject: Q4 budget",
	"digest": "sha256:4f1c2e9a"
}
```

The request shows the summary in the call's place, as data, as it would show the call. Only a write that declares the preview is ever called so, and only to ask its owner: a contract that never promised a preview would take that call for the action itself, and a read before its owner allowed it would read their data. The harness keeps the digest with the frozen call, and the call its owner allowed carries it in `x-twake-preview-digest`, never `x-twake-preview`. A contract that finds what the call acts on changed since answers `409` and does nothing: the harness then tells the owner itself that the action was not done, without the model, which reads in the conversation the call, the contract's answer and that notice. The digest is erased with what the call would send once the call is decided, and the harness logs neither it nor the summary; the gateway's audit records the digest an allowed call carries, which ties that call to the preview its owner saw.

Any other answer to a preview asks the owner nothing. A preview the broker refuses for want of the owner's permission asks for that permission, as a refused call does, and the call is previewed once it may be. Any other success is taken for the call itself, done, since a contract that ignores the header answers that way: the turn ends on the harness's own notice that the application did the action without waiting for the owner's yes, the model reads that the call was made, with the status of the answer and never its body, which may hold a summary meant for the owner, the operator reads an error, and the replica stops previewing that contract until its catalog loads again, so that no other preview there acts. An error, which the model reads to fix the call or tell its owner, and no answer in time, after which the model reads that whether the application did anything is unknown, leave the contract previewed, and the operator reads a warning that says what was wrong, never what the contract answered.

With their own token, and under their own rights as on the other owner routes, `chat` to read, grant and answer and `consents.withdraw_own` to withdraw, an owner manages their consents through the API, as a settings page would: `GET /v1/consents` lists them; `PUT /v1/consents/{application}/{level}` grants a level the catalog offers (201, or 200 when it was given already), and `DELETE /v1/consents/{application}/{level}` withdraws one (204, or 404 when there is none). A turn through `POST /v1/chat` that stops on the harness's question answers with the whole request, as the room shows it, and returns the call it froze as `pending_call` beside it, and a direct call through `POST /v1/tool` answers 202 with that call instead of running; a pending call shows the question alone. `GET /v1/pending-calls` lists what waits for the owner's answer, asked in their room (`room`), in a turn through the API's chat (`api_chat`) or by a direct call through its tool route (`api_tool`): its id, application, level, reasons, the harness's question with what the application and level cover there, and when it expires, never what the call would send nor the model's words alongside it. `POST /v1/pending-calls/{id}/approve` answers yes: a call asked in the room resumes there as after a ✅ (202), a turn through the API goes on and answers as `POST /v1/chat` does, admitted like any turn, the call still waiting if the turn is refused, and a direct tool call runs and answers as `POST /v1/tool` does, under the owner's rights at that moment; the consent a yes to a first use grants is recorded as given through the API. `POST /v1/pending-calls/{id}/refuse` answers no, and in the room the harness says so as after a ❌. An answer through the API is recorded under an id of its own, `api:` and the request's id, as one in the room is under its event's, so that the first answer wins, in the room or through the API, however close they come: a later one through the API gets a 409 with the call's state, and a later one in the room changes nothing. A yes through the API runs a call of a turn through the API, or of a direct tool call, at once, with no job behind it: should its replica die before the call runs, the owner's next yes runs it once the lease a replica holds a job for, fifteen minutes, has passed, and gets a 409 before. Another owner's consents and calls stay out of reach under row-level security: the routes answer 404.

Each role counts on its own `/metrics` what it does of consent, by application (`domain`), `level` and `reason`, never by owner, room or argument; a call that waits for several reasons counts once, under all of them joined by `+`. The api role counts the calls it freezes, `harness_consent_requests_total`, the question of an owner's first brief under the domain `brief`, and the calls it runs once their owner allowed them, `harness_consent_replays_total`, whose `outcome` is `ok` when the application answered with a success and `failed` otherwise. The matrix role counts the owners' answers in the chat, and the api role those given through it, `harness_consent_answers_total`, by `answer` (`yes` or `no`), `via` (`reaction`, `words` or `api`) and `outcome`: `decided`, or `expired` or `superseded` for an answer that came too late and ran nothing. It also counts the questions a newer one superseded, `harness_consent_supersessions_total`, which the api role counts too for the questions and calls a withdrawal closes, and those an answer found past their lifetime, `harness_consent_expiries_total`, in which the api role counts those it finds too, and the questions a brief gave way to or asked in its place that the next brief to go out, or the brief that stops for want of an answer, closes unanswered (see Morning brief), and the worker role the questions its hourly pass expires. A counter shows once it counted, and, like every counter of `/metrics`, is its replica's own: a dashboard sums it over the replicas of the role. A replay or a frozen call retried after a failed commit may be counted twice, by the replica that retries it.
